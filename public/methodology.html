METHODOLOGY

How the estimate works.

Texas Business Worth provides an informational estimate, not a certified appraisal, fairness opinion, offer, tax opinion or investment advice. Actual transaction value can differ materially after financial verification, due diligence and negotiation.

1. Earnings-based valuation

The model starts with owner earnings (SDE) or EBITDA and applies a benchmark multiple appropriate to the selected business category. Earnings are the primary valuation method because private-company buyers generally focus on transferable cash flow.

2. Revenue sanity check

A lower-weight revenue method is used as a reasonableness check. This helps reduce extreme outcomes when an entered earnings figure is unusually high or low relative to revenue. It is not treated as an equal-weight method.

3. Texas market calibration

The model can apply separate calibration for Austin–Round Rock, Dallas–Fort Worth, Houston, San Antonio and the rest of Texas. Hyperlocal data should only receive meaningful weight when the underlying dataset has sufficient observations. When local samples are thin, the system should rely more heavily on broader Texas and national industry data.

4. Business-quality adjustments

Revenue trend, recurring or repeat revenue, customer concentration, management depth, owner dependence, operating history and earnings scale can move the earnings multiple within controlled limits. These adjustments are directional indicators of transferability and buyer risk, not universal appraisal rules.

5. Data versioning

Every stored valuation records the model version, benchmark version, benchmark source, geography and calculation inputs. Licensed transaction databases and industry financial feeds can replace the seed benchmarks without changing the customer experience. Historical estimates retain the methodology used when they were generated.

6. Potential value gap

The value-gap module models a limited set of improvements to weaker business-quality factors. It is an illustrative scenario, not a forecast or guarantee that a buyer would pay the modeled amount.
